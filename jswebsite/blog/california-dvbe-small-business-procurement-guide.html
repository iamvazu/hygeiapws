1. Understanding California's DVBE & SB Mandates

Public agencies, university systems, healthcare districts, and commercial prime contractors throughout Santa Clara County operate under aggressive supplier diversity statutes established by the California State Legislature:

  • California Military and Veterans Code § 999: Establishes a mandatory 3% Disabled Veteran Business Enterprise (DVBE) participation goal across all state-funded purchasing and service contracts.
  • Executive Order S-02-06: Directs state departments to achieve a minimum of 25% small business participation in state contracting.
  • California Public Contract Code § 10115: Enforces good-faith effort documentation and reporting standards on prime bidders, with non-compliant bids subject to immediate disqualification.

For facility directors and procurement managers, satisfying these diversity mandates while maintaining spotless, five-star facility operations requires partnering with a certified firm that possesses genuine enterprise-grade operational capacity. Frequently Asked Questions

What is California's DVBE procurement mandate, and who does it apply to?

California Military and Veterans Code § 999 mandates that all state departments, agencies, university systems (UC/CSU), and public works authorities allocate a minimum of 3% of their annual contract dollars to certified Disabled Veteran Business Enterprises (DVBE). Commercial prime contractors bidding on public projects must also demonstrate DVBE subcontracting participation.

How does partnering with a certified DVBE benefit commercial prime contractors?

Prime contractors bidding on municipal, state, or utility contracts often gain up to a 5% bid evaluation incentive point advantage by including a certified DVBE like Montero Enterprises Inc. dba Hygeia in their subcontracting plan, frequently making the difference between winning and losing competitive bids.

What is the difference between a DVBE and a standard Small Business (SB)?

A Small Business (SB) is certified based on revenue thresholds and employee count under California DGS rules. A DVBE requires that the business be at least 51% owned, managed, and controlled by one or more disabled veterans with service-connected disabilities recognized by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Hygeia holds both certifications.

Is Montero Enterprises registered with the California Department of Industrial Relations (DIR)?

Yes. Montero Enterprises Inc. is registered with the California Department of Industrial Relations under DIR #JS-LR-100 132 4606, allowing us to perform prevailing wage maintenance and public works janitorial contracts across California.
